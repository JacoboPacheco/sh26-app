0. SPEC.md exists, or PHASE ≥ 2 → change nothing: "/ideas is kickoff-only — the idea is already chosen (PHASE n)." CLAUDE.md → Idea already filled (a second run) → it is a candidate too, and the `RUNNER-UP` and `Facts:` lines get replaced, not appended.
1. PHASE 0 → do PLAYBOOK Phase 0 first. Read CLAUDE.md → "Sponsor / company challenges to target", and in `.claude/skills/spec/SKILL.md` the "what wins here" paragraph (above its step 0) and the sponsor rule with its claim tests (step 2). The sponsor list says `not released yet` (PLAYBOOK Phase 0) → write `Sponsors: unknown` on every card and skip step 6's no-honest-card line. If the list arrives later in the run: paste it in (PLAYBOOK Phase 0), redo only the Sponsors lines and the no-honest-card line — scores don't change — and re-issue the pick; a pick call interrupted by the paste doesn't count toward the three.
2. **Interview: one AskUserQuestion, three or four questions, no recommendation, options in no particular order.**
   - Q1, the signal. Per idea the human brought (up to two — more than two → score the first two and say so), one question digging into its domain: "Doing that, what goes wrong or wastes time?" with options a slow step · a common mistake · something tracked by hand · something nobody can see. No idea brought: "What annoyed you or someone near you this month?" with options my job · my classes · a hobby · something at home. Either way the text says "pick Other and type the actual thing; the options are only prompts".
   - Q2, multi-select facts: first hackathon · phone at the table · can print a prop · own sample data or files.
   - Q3, what they'd rather demo, as what the judge sees: the judge gives it an input and gets a result · the screen reacts live to the judge · a before/after · a map, timeline, or chart fills in. At least two of the four cards use the shape they pick.
3. **Generate 8–10 one-line candidates internally, then cull to 4 cards** (≤ 100 words each; the human's may run to 130) plus one line each, at most 4, under "also considered — cut because …" — cut reasons are structural (data not public, needs hardware, third input of the same type), never "already exists": nothing has been searched yet. The first two ideas the human brought (`$ARGUMENTS`, then answers) are extra cards, scored and searched like the rest; one that is vague or infeasible as stated is made concrete from their Q1 answer, with "read as: …" on the card; a generated candidate that is their idea with a twist goes on their card as "twist: …", not a separate one. Rules:
   - Build only from what CLAUDE.md → Stack and "How this codebase is wired" already provide, plus libraries that pip-install from wheels on Windows and Linux with no system install (live updates = polling). That list is a constraint, not a menu: no more than two generated cards share an input type (a webcam counts as photo input). Nothing that needs hardware, scraping, a paid API, real people's private data, third-party approval, visiting untrusted URLs, or a dataset you can't produce in an hour.
   - Before writing a card, name the project you'd expect in any hackathon gallery for that problem. If your pitch sentence describes that project too, add a specific twist or drop the candidate.
   - The technical piece must be something a chat window can't do: a decode, a measurement, a cross-check, a live loop, a transformation. "Gemini looks at it and explains" alone is out.
   - Demo format: the "what wins here" paragraph. The judge moment is watched within about 10 seconds of the judge's own action, using only the props Q2 says the human has; a card that needs a phone names that risk or uses the laptop webcam.
   - Sponsors: `/spec`'s sponsor rule and its claim tests, applied per card. Coverage is a result, not a goal — never generate a card to cover a track.
   Card format: **Name** — one-sentence pitch · For: … · Judge watches: … · Hard part: … · Gallery default: … · Sponsors: … (one "because" each, or none) · Skeleton by K+10 (≈ 9 hours of building): yes / risky — what sinks it.
4. **Score every card on the five judging criteria, 1–5.** Completion: buildable by K+10 by shcht with the human answering questions — 5 = every piece is in the starter or a pip wheel and the demo path is ≤ 3 screens, 4 = one new piece to learn; a card that says "risky" caps at 3; a card that depends on a sponsor API the starter doesn't wire is "risky", not out — a Gemini call through `llm.py` is risky only if the judge moment has no committed fallback result; more than 3 new screens on the demo path, or a library that needs a system install or native build, is a 2; 1–2 is out regardless of total. Originality: blank until step 5. Design: 5 = the judge reacts within 10 seconds of their own action, 4 = reacts after a one-sentence setup, 3 = watched but flat, 2 = watched only after more than a sentence of setup, 1 = explained. Technology: 2 = CRUD, or one frame or file sent to a model and shown as-is, 3 = a model call whose output code then aggregates or thresholds; non-model arithmetic; or a result computed ahead of time that the judge only selects, 4 = a measurement or cross-check the model can't do alone, 5 = a 4 whose core is a named algorithm (an alignment, a homography, a clustering, an optimisation), not a threshold on one number. A card whose technical piece a chat window can do is out. Practicality: 5 = the named person would switch from what they use today, 4 = they'd add it alongside what they use, 3 = they'd try it once, 2 = they'd look once, 1 = nobody; a legal or compliance blocker for that person caps it at 3.
5. **Originality search:** for every card not out, two queries — one for the gallery default (it confirms the default and finds the free tools) and one for the card's own twist. WebSearch results are enough; WebFetch only on devpost.com pages — anything else prompts. Score the card's own pitch, twist included: 1 = it already exists, 2 = a close match differs only in audience or input, 3 = the problem is well covered and the twist is small, or a chat window does the twist in one message — a manual procedure for the twist doesn't lower the score, only a tool that does it does — 4 = nothing relevant found, 5 = results exist for the problem but none has the twist. A card whose pitch already exists, or that is worse than a free tool the named person already has, is out.
6. **Table and verdict:** all cards with totals; ties: Originality, then Completion, then the card using Q3's shape. Recommend the top total among cards that remove the Q1 pain for the person who had it — an off-pain card only if it leads by 3 or more — with its single biggest risk and the cheapest mitigation (a committed sample input in `backend/demo/`, a manual fallback path). One line each on what is out and why. One line on tracks with no honest card, and why.
7. **Pick — one AskUserQuestion, two questions.** (a) Which to build — at most 4 options, filled in this order, skipping duplicates: the recommended card; the human's card(s), marked "out: <killer>" if out; the runner-up (the best surviving card that isn't the recommendation); "Regenerate" (a bare click = new problems from the same answers; the change goes in Other), first round only and only if `date` + 12 minutes ≤ K+0:40; a combine of two, only if it beats both, scored like a card first. Anything that doesn't fit is named in the question text: "or type it in Other". Other text: names a card or a project → that is the pick (a project not on the table is scored per steps 4–5 and treated as the human's card); describes a change → Regenerate with it if available, else "you pick"; a sponsor list → step 1. (b) "Which would you rather explain to ten judges?" — the top two surviving cards in random order, no recommendation. (b) is a check, not a vote: if it names a different card than (a), say one plain line — "You picked X but would rather explain Y — building X; say 'Y' to switch" — and build X; a regenerate or combine answer ignores (b). Fewer than two survivors → (b) is skipped and (a) is the survivors, the human's card(s), Regenerate if available, then the best out cards with their killers, 4 in all; zero survivors → recommend Regenerate if available, else the out card with the highest Completion if it is ≥ 3, else say nothing is buildable and ask for an idea in Other. "You pick" or "none" (and at the re-pick, another regenerate) means the recommended option of the (a) being answered — say so. **Regenerate** reruns steps 3–6 with the change applied on top of the Q answers: earlier cards are not regenerated or rescored, and the re-pick's (a) still offers the human's card at its first-round score; no also-considered lines. If the new top card totals below the old one, say so — the old one is the re-pick's recommended card. The re-pick's (a) has no Regenerate option.
